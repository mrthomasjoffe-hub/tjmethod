The part that gets skipped

The technology lands. The strategy lands. The people are the variable nobody resourced.

Change rarely fails in the plan. It fails in the eight weeks either side of go-live, in the gap between what an executive team decided in March and what a shift supervisor understands on a Tuesday in October.

That gap is not a mystery. It is a resourcing decision. The people work is genuinely large. Done properly it means naming every group the change touches, sizing the impact on each one, measuring where they stand before you move, drafting comms that answer the question they are actually asking rather than the one you wish they were asking, and reporting all of it upward in a form a steering committee will accept.

That is six weeks of senior practitioner time. Most programmes do not have six weeks spare, so they produce the deck and quietly skip the rest. Then everyone is surprised when adoption is the problem.

The Foundry exists so the human side is not the part that gets skipped.

Before you read a single feature

Tick the ones that are true this week.

Be honest rather than generous. If three or more of these land, the issue is not your project plan. It is that the people side of your change has no engine behind it.

  1. 01

    Change and comms is one line on a slide.

    There is a box on the plan and a name next to it. Underneath there is no strategy document, no impact register, no measurement baseline and no stakeholder map. The box is real. The work behind it is not yet.

  2. 02

    Nobody can tell you how many people this actually touches.

    Ask for headcount by impacted group and you get a range, a guess and a promise to come back to you. That number quietly sets your training cost, your comms channels, your support model and your go-live risk. You are running without it.

  3. 03

    You know who will block this, and you have not had the conversation.

    The stakeholder analysis lives in your head as a feeling about two or three names. Because it has never been written down, it has never been worked, and nobody else in the programme can help you carry it.

  4. 04

    The SteerCo pack gets built the night before.

    Every cycle, the same nine slides, reassembled by hand from a plan, a risk log and an inbox. Hours of your most expensive time spent producing a document instead of preparing a decision.

  5. 05

    Comms arrive after the rumour, not before it.

    People hear about the change from a corridor, a contractor or a partner manager. By the time the official message lands it is a correction, and you have spent trust you were going to need at cutover.

  6. 06

    You will find out whether people were ready on the day you can no longer do anything about it.

    Readiness gets measured at go-live, or not at all. With no baseline there is no way to prove the work moved anything, and no way to catch the one cohort that is going backwards while the average looks fine.

  7. 07

    The last change is still in the room.

    Two enterprise systems landed in the past eighteen months and drew on the same audiences. The appetite for another one is low. Nothing in the plan accounts for that, so the plan is optimistic by default.

  8. 08

    Consultation is a legal obligation nobody has diarised.

    Some of these people are covered by an enterprise agreement. Major workplace change triggers a consultation clause, with a notice period that started before the decision was locked in. Someone will remember that in week nine.

What The Foundry does about it

Every symptom above has a mechanism behind it.

Not a template library. An engine that takes one structured intake and produces the whole set, then regenerates all of it the moment the change changes.

Symptom 01No people plan behind the swimlane.

The change strategy and plan.

Describe the organisation, the change and the people it touches. The Foundry produces the full strategy document, phased into Prepare, Manage and Sustain, with focus activities for each phase and cutover criteria for each site. Export it as a Word document and it is ready for governance review.

Symptom 02The headcount is a guess.

Real names underneath every number.

Upload a CSV export from payroll or HR. You get a people register with site, employment type, enterprise agreement coverage and group assignment for every individual. Tag your champions, your UAT testers and your influencers. Then check that every impacted group has real people behind its headcount before a single message goes out.

Symptom 03The blockers live in your head.

Stakeholder analysis, written down and prioritised.

Influence against impact, current stance, change maturity and agreement coverage, with the engine's read on who to prioritise and why. A high-influence detractor raises a flag and a recommended sponsor conversation inside two weeks. The same grid appears in the SteerCo pack, so the conversation you have been avoiding becomes an agenda item instead of a worry.

Symptom 04The night-before deck.

A nine-slide SteerCo pack, built from live project state.

Decisions required, risks with mitigations, the stakeholder grid, metrics against baseline. Exported as native PowerPoint minutes before the meeting. This alone replaces hours of deck assembly every single cycle.

Symptom 05Comms behind the rumour.

A drafted communications pack, in your organisation's voice.

Audience by audience, channel by channel, sequenced against your go-live date, including the questions people will actually ask with answers a line manager can give. Four built-in tones ship with The Foundry, or build your own from a brand pack, a tone-of-voice document or a set of example comms. Deskless cohorts get channels that reach them rather than an email address they do not have.

Symptom 06No readiness measurement.

Baseline, pulse and post-implementation, against the same yardstick.

Two ready-to-field instruments, channel-appropriate distribution, and a measurement cadence anchored to your go-live date. Underneath sits a four-category metrics framework covering adoption and usage, delivery effectiveness, engagement and readiness, and business outcomes. Every metric carries a baseline, a target, a cadence and a named owner.

Symptom 07Fatigue nobody planned for.

Change fatigue is an input, not an excuse.

Tell The Foundry what landed in the past eighteen months. It changes the risk flags, the sequencing and the framing of every message, because a worn-down workforce needs to hear what they gain on day one, not what the system does.

Symptom 08The consultation clock.

Risk flags you did not have to think of first.

Enterprise agreement coverage triggers a consultation workstream at least ten weeks out, with the obligations spelled out and the sessions minuted. Heavily impacted groups with unmeasured sentiment trigger a baseline survey. The Foundry raises these before you do, which is the entire point of it.

Underneath all of itWhat is changing, for whom, and how much.

The change impact register, built three ways.

Capture person-to-person interviews with a generated interview script, compare as-is and to-be documents and let the engine draft the impacts it finds, or add them by hand. Edit the title, category, scale and perception on each draft, then keep it or discard it. This is the assessment most programmes never finish, and it is the one that decides your training and support model.
